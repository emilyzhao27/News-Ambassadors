About News Ambassadors

Journalism with communities,
not just about them.

News Ambassadors enlists college journalism students to help local newsrooms fill gaps in coverage of underreported communities. We support students to do rigorously reported stories uplifting solutions and common ground, training them on specific tools including Solutions Journalism and depolarization reporting strategies known as Complicating the Narratives.

Students then report stories informed by these strategies and by input from community members themselves, so stories are responsive to local needs and information gaps rather than journalists' assumptions about what news people need. The top stories are shared with local newsroom partners for vetting and possible broadcast beyond campus.

Project History

How we got here.

2020

Concept developed during COVID-19 pandemic polarization and media turmoil. Incubated as part of InterAct's Digital Citizen Project, connecting Americans to leaders and each other since 1998.

2021

Series of short audio pieces with student reporters in five states on COVID-19 regional differences across the US. Watch the overview.

Fall 2022

Inaugural pilot with the University of Missouri School of Journalism and Columbia University's Graduate School of Journalism. First stories resulted in a KBIA segment on affordable housing across Moberly, MO and Brooklyn, NY.

2023 to present

Added Solutions Journalism and Complicating the Narratives frameworks. By 2024-2025, the program grew to 70 students across 10 university campuses alongside 4 public radio partners, offering strategies as 3 distinct tracks while encouraging participants to take more than one.

Our Frameworks

Three evidence-based approaches to better journalism.

Framework 01

Solutions Journalism

Solutions Journalism is rigorous reporting on responses to social problems. It goes beyond identifying what is broken to show what is working and why, applying the same skeptical standards as traditional reporting.

This is not "feel good" journalism. It asks: what is the response, how effective is it, and what are its limitations? The result is reporting that informs and inspires rather than leaving audiences overwhelmed.

Originally designed for journalists, the framework is also beneficial for those in education, public policy, governance, and international development who want to build local resilience and problem-solving capacity.

The Four Pillars

Response

A specific, defined response to an existing social problem

Evidence

Proof of how the response worked, or why it didn't, with data

Limitations

What the approach does not address or got wrong

Insight

Lessons learned to help others consider similar approaches

Framework 02

Depolarization Reporting Strategies

Also known as Complicating the Narratives

Our training centers on the Complicating the Narratives method, codesigned by High Conflict author Amanda Ripley and her Good Conflict colleague Helene Biandudi Hofer.

This method is born from researching conflict mediation psychology studies: how people actually behave when feeling suspicious and threatened by the other side. It distills lessons journalists can use to improve coverage of contentious issues.

Traditional journalism frames every conflict as a binary: two sides, equally weighted, winner take all. This framing amplifies polarization. Complicating the Narratives helps reporters find genuine human complexity underneath the surface.

Core Techniques

Looping

A mediation practice: reflect back exactly what the source said before asking the next question, confirming understanding and signaling genuine listening.

The 22 Questions

Amanda Ripley's questions designed to complicate stories and draw out nuance, revealing what people on all sides actually care about and where they feel unheard. View the full list.

The Onion Model

Moving from surface positions to underlying needs, fears, and shared values to understand why people hold the positions they do.

Framework 03

Community Engagement Reporting

News Ambassadors provides Community Engagement training and mentorship support for news-academic partnerships, community surveys, listening sessions, and Information Ecosystem Assessment reports.

This approach involves local communities in shaping newsroom priorities, story selection, and framing. It is journalism done with communities, not just about them, shifting from "we cover you" to "we work with you."

We believe journalists who layer these three methods together improve their ability to support resilient local communities that can move beyond us-versus-them thinking and come together to address shared challenges.

2025 Evaluation Results

What the data shows.

In 2024-2025, 70 students across 10 university campuses completed News Ambassadors training. An independent evaluation measured knowledge, confidence, and career motivation before and after.

Awareness before and after training (4-point scale)

Key findings

Knowledge gains are significant

Students showed large increases in awareness of both Solutions Journalism (1.7 to 3.7) and Depolarization strategies (2.0 to 3.0). 94-100% reported increased readiness to apply Looping and the 22 Questions in their reporting.

Skills translate directly to practice

92% said they were likely or highly likely to apply News Ambassadors frameworks in their future reporting. Fellows described gaining concrete tools: the Solutions Journalism story tracker, community listening techniques, and ways to identify coverage gaps.

Training deepens career commitment

88% of mentored students said learning Solutions Journalism increased their interest in journalism careers. 100% of cross-site exchange participants said the experience furthered their understanding and was engaging and useful.
