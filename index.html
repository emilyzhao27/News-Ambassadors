About News Ambassadors

Journalism with communities,
not just about them.

News Ambassadors enlists college journalism students to help local newsrooms fill gaps in coverage of underreported communities. We support students to do rigorously reported stories uplifting solutions and common ground, training them on specific tools including Solutions Journalism and depolarization reporting strategies known as Complicating the Narratives.

Students then report stories informed by these strategies and by input from community members themselves, so stories are responsive to local needs and information gaps rather than journalists' assumptions about what news people need. The top stories are shared with local newsroom partners for vetting and possible broadcast beyond campus.

Meet the Director

Shia Levitt

Shia Levitt is a longtime public radio journalist and journalism educator who has reported from East and West Africa, Asia and the Middle East for NPR, Marketplace, and other outlets. She is an accredited trainer of both CTN and Solutions Journalism, and director and creator of News Ambassadors. Our Frameworks

Three evidence-based approaches to better journalism.

Framework 01

Solutions Journalism

Solutions Journalism is rigorous reporting on solutions to wide-facing social problems. Our training focuses on the four pillars reporters strive to include in solutions-focused stories: Response to an existing problem; Evidence of how the response worked (or why it didn't); Limitations to the approach; and Insight or lessons learned to help others considering whether to try similar approaches in their own communities and contexts.

The Four Pillars

1. Response

A response to an existing problem

2. Evidence

Evidence of how the response worked (or why it didn't)

3. Limitations

Limitations to the approach

4. Insight

Insight or lessons learned to help others considering whether to try similar approaches in their own communities and contexts

Framework 02

Depolarization Reporting Strategies

Also known as Complicating the Narratives

Our Depolarization Reporting Strategies training focuses on the four pillars of the Complicating the Narratives method, designed by High Conflict author Amanda Ripley and her Good Conflict colleague Hélène Biandudi Hofer.

The strategies were born from researching conflict mediation psychology studies: how people actually behave when they're feeling suspicious and threatened by the other side, and distilling lessons that can help improve reporting and narrative storytelling on contentious issues. Originally designed for journalists covering polarizing issues, these tools have applications for many different types of reporting as well as far beyond the journalism field.

We also pull in a few other relevant strategies and tools to enhance the training. Trainees will learn tools and receive resources they can immediately apply to their work and lives today to help move beyond "Us versus Them" framing and tell stronger stories of "we."

Core Techniques

Looping

A mediation practice: reflect back exactly what the source said before asking the next question, confirming understanding and signaling genuine listening.

The 22 Questions

Amanda Ripley's questions designed to complicate stories and draw out nuance, revealing what people on all sides actually care about and where they feel unheard.

Framework 03

Community Engagement Reporting

News Ambassadors provides Community Engagement training and mentorship support for news-academic partnerships, community surveys, listening sessions, and Information Ecosystem Assessment reports.

This approach involves local communities in shaping newsroom priorities, story selection, and framing. It is journalism done with communities, not just about them, shifting from "we cover you" to "we work with you."

We believe journalists who layer these three methods together improve their ability to support resilient local communities that can move beyond us-versus-them thinking and come together to address shared challenges.

2025 Evaluation Results

What the data shows.

In 2024-2025, 70 students across 10 university campuses completed News Ambassadors training. An independent evaluation measured knowledge, confidence, and career motivation before and after.

Awareness before and after training (4-point scale)

Key findings

Knowledge gains are significant

Students showed large increases in awareness of both Solutions Journalism (1.7 to 3.7) and Depolarization strategies (2.0 to 3.0). 94-100% reported increased readiness to apply Looping and the 22 Questions in their reporting.

Skills translate directly to practice

92% said they were likely or highly likely to apply News Ambassadors frameworks in their future reporting. Fellows described gaining concrete tools: the Solutions Journalism story tracker, community listening techniques, and ways to identify coverage gaps.

Training deepens career commitment

88% of mentored students said learning Solutions Journalism increased their interest in journalism careers. 100% of cross-site exchange participants said the experience furthered their understanding and was engaging and useful.
